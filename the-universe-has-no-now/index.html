Synopsis

Two clocks. Two places. One moment. It feels like the simplest idea in the world — and it is not true.

The film starts with the most innocent question in physics — are these two events happening at the same time? — and follows it, honestly, as far as our best theories can take it: from the relativity of simultaneity and the geometry of spacetime, through the speed of light as the ruler of the universe, to gravity as curvature, a ray-traced Schwarzschild black hole, the probabilistic world of quantum mechanics, entanglement (and what it cannot do), and finally the open problem: general relativity and quantum field theory are each confirmed to absurd precision, and they still do not fit together.

Its conclusion is not "quantum physics is weird". It is that we possess extraordinarily precise descriptions of different parts of reality and, as yet, no single consistent description of all of it.

Methodology

Computed, not painted

Every frame is rendered from code. The black hole is a Schwarzschild ray tracer: null geodesics integrated from the Binet equation, a Keplerian thin disk with Shakura–Sunyaev emission, Doppler + gravitational shift (g³ beaming), lensed background stars. The lensing scene applies the point-mass lens equation pixel by pixel; the atomic clouds are random samples of the exact hydrogen |ψ|²; the two-slit screen accumulates single detections drawn from |ψ₁+ψ₂|².

Science first

Every narrated claim is tagged in docs/science-matrix.md as established established or research active research, with sources in SOURCES.md. Physics tests check the simulations (critical impact parameter 3√3 M, weak-field deflection 4M/b, orbital normalisation, Lorentz values shown on screen).

Picture follows voice

Narration is synthesised sentence by sentence; shot durations are derived from the measured audio, so captions are exact and the rhythm (pauses, silences) is editorial, not accidental.

Sound

Procedural score (detuned-sine pads, a two-note "clock" motif, drones, glass textures, a tritone for the clash) and a deliberate sound design of interfaces and transitions — no explosions in space. Mixed to −14 LUFS, true peak below −1 dBTP.
